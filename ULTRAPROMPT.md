# ULTRAPROMPT.md — Master Build Prompt: Emirati Cold-Outreach Engine

You are building the **Emirati Cold-Outreach Engine** from scratch, working from this document plus `PLAN.md` in this repo. This document is your contract. Read it fully before writing a line of code. Where this document and `PLAN.md` conflict, this document wins (it incorporates edge-case findings that amend the plan — e.g. the OAuth scope set).

## 1. Mission & Product Context

**What it is.** A single-user (v1) web tool that lands job interviews for an Emirati candidate via personalized cold email, exploiting the structural advantage that UAE Emiratisation quotas and the Nafis program create: most large UAE employers have hiring quotas for Emiratis, salary subsidies for hiring them, and often a dedicated Emiratisation/Nafis program lead whose KPI is literally to hire people like your user. The tool finds companies and the right people inside them, gathers verifiable evidence for personalization, drafts short human-sounding emails, and runs a polite, persistent follow-up cadence — with a human approving every single send.

**Who the user is.** A lazy, tech-shy Emirati job seeker (initially: the founder's friend), on their phone, with a personal Gmail account. They will not read documentation, will not debug anything, and will abandon the product at the first confusing screen. Every flow must be embarrassingly easy: connect email in one click, answer a few questions, review, tap Send. If a step feels like "setup," it is designed wrong. The minimal UI is a v1 requirement, not polish.

**The founder** operates the sourcing pipeline manually behind the scenes and debugs at midnight. The same build is the seed of a commercial product, so unit-economics telemetry and a globally-keyed schema matter from send #1.

**The three subsystems:**
1. **Data collection** — companies → people → *evidence* (raw-source facts with URLs, for personalization). Sourced via a four-tier pyramid: Tier 1 regulatory/official registers (DFSA, ADGM/FSRA, DFM/ADX disclosures, gov org pages, WAM) → Tier 2 the company's own pages (leadership, press, Emiratisation/careers pages) → Tier 3 search-engine-indexed LinkedIn (`site:linkedin.com/in` queries via Google/Bing — never touching LinkedIn itself) → Tier 4 manual human LinkedIn browsing with a paste-in capture form. Work top-down, stop at the first tier that yields the right person; record the tier on every contact. Email addresses are pattern-inferred (first.last@domain dominates UAE corporates) and verified via a NeverBounce/ZeroBounce-class API.
2. **Email generation** — structured evidence + template → draft, under a **clarify-&-refuse contract**: the generator receives only structured evidence rows (each with a source URL) and may not state any fact not present in them; thin or stale evidence produces a refusal plus a collection request, never a hallucination. Template: personal hook (highest-tier usable evidence) → 2-sentence intro from the user's intro-block library → the Emirati/Nafis angle (plain to HR/Emiratisation leads; at most a factual closer to hiring managers; never the opener) → one ask (coffee chat by default) → sign-off. Under 120 words, no attachments, banned-phrase lint (no "I hope this email finds you well", "I came across", "resonated", "excited to", "delve", "leverage", "passionate about", no em dashes), versioned templates, subject lines short and identity-led ("Emirati [university] grad").
3. **Outreach state machine** — UAE-working-day countdowns (Sat–Sun weekend, UAE public holidays as shiftable windows, late-Ramadan pause), sequence = email 1 → +4 working days → +5 more working days (breakup), person rotation up a per-company ladder of 2–4 contacts with ~5-working-day cooldown, holiday-aware warm openers, reply detection by polling Gmail. Tone target, in the founder's words: *grit and continuous chasing, but never annoying.*

**The three screens:**
- **Screen 1 — Onboard** (once, <10 min): one-click Gmail OAuth; chip-based profile interview (roles, industries, cities, education, one concrete credibility marker, tone); hygiene questions (employers/rejections/never-contact); identity block (send-as address, signature, name spelling); CV generation or upload. Then the system builds the queue behind the scenes.
- **Screen 2 — Review & Send** (daily core loop): the email exactly as it will send (editable) beside/above an **evidence panel** — every personalized claim annotated with the raw-source quote and its URL so the user can judge in 10 seconds that the personalization is real. One button: **Send**, via their own Gmail through the API. Follow-ups and reply-assist drafts flow through the same queue. No auto-send exists anywhere.
- **Screen 3 — Dashboard**: companies reached, per-company timeline (who/when/which step), next actions with working-day countdowns, reply/bounce status per thread, pause and "I got the job" controls.

**Success bar** before commercializing: ≥8–10% reply rate and ≥2 interviews or serious intro calls within 6 weeks for the friend.

## 2. The POV Directive

This is a standing instruction that applies to **every feature you build, before you build it**:

**Role-play the three personas first.**
1. **The tech-shy user on their phone.** Walk the flow as someone who has never seen it, is mildly intimidated by technology, reads only the biggest text on screen, and will close the tab at the first sign of jargon, an English error code, or a Google scare screen. Every screen must pass this test: *"Would a first-time, non-technical user get through this without help?"* If the answer requires "well, they'd just need to…", the answer is no. Redesign.
2. **The busy UAE hiring manager receiving the email.** Read every generated artifact — subject, body, follow-up, reply-assist draft — as a senior person at a UAE company with 200 unread emails, cultural expectations about address and honorifics, and colleagues two desks away who compare notes. Would this read as human, specific, and respectful? Would the follow-up feel like grit or like a bot? Would two emails from this tool ever embarrass each other?
3. **The founder debugging at midnight.** For every state transition, ask: *"What does the user see when this goes wrong?"* and *"What do I see in the logs, and can I fix it without raw SQL?"* Every error state needs a calm, plain-language, user-facing rendering (never SMTP jargon, never a stack trace, never a silent failure) and an append-only log entry that makes the failure reconstructable.

**Hunt for new edge cases actively.** The register in §5 is a floor, not a ceiling. While building each feature, deliberately probe: what if the user does this twice? on two devices? after two weeks away? in Arabic? during Eid? with a revoked token? Every NEW edge case you find — beyond the register — must be added, **with its solution or explicit deferral**, to an `EDGE_CASES.md` ledger in the repo root, in the same format as §5. That ledger is a living deliverable of this build, reviewed at every milestone.

## 3. Hard Rules

These are inviolable. Enforce them **in code** (schema constraints, invariant checks at send time, absent code paths), not in comments or UI copy alone.

From `PLAN.md` §10, verbatim:

1. **No auto-send** — every email is human-approved on the Review screen.
2. **No fabricated personalization** — every claim traces to an evidence row with a source URL.
3. **Verified emails only.**
4. **Volume ceiling: 10–15 first-emails/day per user** (mailbox warmup + realistic evidence throughput).
5. **One live sequence per company**; ladder rotation per PLAN §4; company dormancy after exhaustion.
6. **Countdown in UAE working days**; holiday-aware rescheduling per PLAN §5.
7. **No LinkedIn automation, ever.** Tier 4 is human-only with a capture form.

Additional hard rules forced by the edge-case register:

8. **Idempotent sends.** Every send goes through a compare-and-swap status transition (`approved → sending`) with our own RFC822 Message-ID persisted *before* the Gmail call; never blind-retry — probe `rfc822msgid:` first. Unique index on `(person_id, step)`. A send can be lost to a crash but never duplicated.
9. **Reply stops everything — after classification.** Every inbound message is classified before it may change state. A human reply supersedes all queued/drafted/approved outreach for that person atomically, and the send-time gate re-checks the thread for unseen inbound inside the CAS — the poll race can lose; the send gate cannot. Auto-replies (OOO, auto-acks, gateway challenges, bounces) are *not* replies and follow their own paths.
10. **No send while blind.** No email may send while `last_successful_poll_at` is older than 6 hours or while the Gmail connection state is not `connected`.
11. **Suppression is checked before drafting, is permanent, and scopes to domains** where required. `removal_request` suppresses person + company; `complaint_escalation` suppresses the domain. Suppression survives dormancy resets and future users.
12. **A classified rejection kills the company ladder**, not just the person. Ladder rotation is only legal after silence.
13. **SQLite is the only draft store.** Gmail Drafts is never used; MIME is constructed at click time from the SQLite copy; the sent text is stored verbatim.
14. **No attachments on any cold-sequence step.** The outreach MIME builder has no attachment path; attachments exist only in reply-assist (warm threads).
15. **All date math is Asia/Dubai calendar dates**, computed by one pure `nextDue()` function; `scheduled_date` is always derived, never authoritative, and is recomputed nightly and on calendar edits.
16. **One org, one live sequence.** All per-company invariants enforce at the `org_group` (normalized-domain) level, and never two sends to the same domain within 48 hours, including across ladder rotations.

## 4. Architecture & Stack

**TypeScript end-to-end.** One language for UI, API routes, and pipeline jobs; one deploy.

- **Next.js** — the three screens + API routes. Mobile-first: the target user is phone-only (the split-view evidence panel becomes inline highlights + bottom sheet on mobile; see register §Review & Send).
- **SQLite** via Prisma or Drizzle — single-file DB, easy backup. It is the system of record for everything, including drafts.
- **Gmail API** — OAuth with **exactly two scopes: `gmail.send` + `gmail.readonly`** (`gmail.compose` is dropped per the register — the SQLite-only draft store makes it unnecessary and shrinks the CASA audit surface). One Gmail client wrapper with exponential backoff + jitter and a per-user token bucket. Poll every 10–15 minutes via `threads.get` on stored thread IDs (catches SPAM/TRASH-labeled replies), for every thread ever sent, forever. Move the OAuth app to **production publishing status before user #1** and start Google verification early.
- **Claude API** (Anthropic SDK) — evidence structuring (with sensitivity labels, entity-resolution cues, translation), the clarify-&-refuse draft generator, the inbound-message classifier (with Arabic/bilingual few-shots), the interview micro-follow-up, and reply-assist drafting. Every Claude call operates under the evidence contract: facts only from evidence rows or confirmed profile fields.
- **Scheduler** — a **stateless, idempotent sweep** (cron-invoked), never a tick: "for every non-terminal outreach past its derived due date with no next-step row, create/refresh the draft; for every sequence past cooldown, advance the ladder." Running twice is a no-op; running late converges to the same state via the stale-redraft rule.

**Data model.** Start from `PLAN.md` §9 and extend it with what the register forces. The schema is **global-keyed from day one**: `company` (unique canonical domain) and `person` (unique normalized email) are user-agnostic; per-user state lives in `user_company_state` and `outreach`; every send writes the `contact_ledger`.

## 5. The Edge-Case Register

**This register is a contract.** Every item below must be either handled in code (with a test where the item describes a state-machine or date behavior) or explicitly logged in `EDGE_CASES.md` as deferred **with a reason**. None may be silently ignored. Items marked critical must all be handled in v1.

### Onboarding

- **Google "unverified app" warning kills onboarding at step one** (critical) — the tech-shy user hits Google's red-triangle "unverified app / wants to read and send your email" screen and closes the tab forever. → Solution: pre-consent screen in-app showing a screenshot of exactly what Google will display, with plain-language scope explanation; request only `gmail.send` + `gmail.readonly` (drop `gmail.compose` — see draft-store rule); move the OAuth app to production publishing status before the first real user and start verification early.
- **Positive replies ask for a CV that doesn't exist** (critical, seen by 3 lenses) — ~half of positive replies say "send your CV"; the fresh-grad user faces a blank document at the highest-stakes moment and stalls for days. → Solution: onboarding generates a one-page PDF CV from the interview answers (education, credibility marker, roles) for glance-approval, or accepts an upload; store it versioned. Reply classifier category `document_request` pre-drafts an in-thread reply with the CV already attached, flagged urgent at the top of the Review queue. Coach rule baked in: never send Emirates ID by email.
- **One-word interview answers produce an unusable profile** (high) — "roles: anything, industries: business, credibility: im hardworking" gives the generator nothing, so every draft is generic or refused. → Solution: chip-based interview (curated UAE role list, city toggles, industry cards); the credibility marker gets a Claude micro-interview that asks exactly one concrete follow-up when vague; enforce a minimum-viable-profile gate before onboarding completes. The generator's refuse contract must never fire on a thin profile, only thin evidence.
- **User oversells in the interview and gets called on it in a reply** (high) — "led a team of 20" (a 2-week stint) goes into every intro; a manager probes, the user panics and ghosts. → Solution: interview asks one verification-framing question ("if they ask about this in a reply, what would you say?"); on hesitation, Claude offers a defensible softer phrasing. Hard rule: the generator may phrase the credibility marker at or below the confirmed specificity, never embellish upward. Profile answers become evidence rows (source = "interview answer") so the no-fact-outside-evidence rule covers self-claims too.
- **Partial consent: user unchecks the send scope** (high) — Google's granular consent lets the user uncheck "Send email"; OAuth still succeeds and the failure surfaces days later as a 403 mid-loop. → Solution: inspect granted scopes in the OAuth callback; if `gmail.send` or `gmail.readonly` is missing, block onboarding completion with a plain-language explanation and re-launch auth. Never let a user reach the Review screen with a token that can't send.
- **Queue auto-targets the user's employer, a rejecting company, or the family firm** (high, seen by 2 lenses) — sourcing runs "behind the scenes" and drafts to companies with real social cost in the dense UAE network. → Solution: onboarding asks three hygiene questions (current/recent employers, recent applications/rejections, never-contact list) → `company.blocked_reason`, matched by domain family at queue-build time; blocked companies shown greyed on the dashboard, unblockable only deliberately. Every draft card also has "Skip this company" with reasons that feed the blocklist; "know someone there" offers the warm-intro reframe.
- **Identity block: name spelling, send-as alias, and signature** (medium) — the romanized name appears three ways (from-line "mohd shamsi 98" vs signature "Mohammed Al Shamsi"), the default send-as may be an unverified alias, and API sends carry no Gmail signature. → Solution: one onboarding step: call `sendAs.list`, offer only verified addresses, store the chosen `sendAsEmail`; show Gmail display name beside the interview name and confirm one canonical signature spelling (deep-link to fix a bad Google display name); pull the signature via `sendAs.get`, confirm it, treat it as a fixed sign-off block outside the 120-word budget, re-fetch weekly.

### Gmail Integration & OAuth

- **Testing-mode refresh tokens die after 7 days and the app silently breaks** (critical, seen by 2 lenses) — Google expires testing-status tokens weekly; on day 8 polling fails silently, sends 401, and follow-ups vanish during the exact week they come due. → Solution: move to production publishing status before user #1 (removes 7-day expiry even unverified). Independently: `connection_state` column (connected | expired | revoked); any `invalid_grant` flips it, blocks sends, and shows one full-width "Reconnect Gmail — nothing is lost" banner on all three screens; proactively surface reconnect on day 6 of token age while in testing.
- **Follow-ups don't thread for the recipient** (critical) — sending with only `threadId` threads in the sender's mailbox, but the recipient's client threads by headers; follow-up 1 arrives as an orphan cold email pretending to be a follow-up. → Solution: generate our own RFC822 Message-ID at first send and store it plus Gmail threadId/messageId; every follow-up MIME sets `In-Reply-To`, full `References` chain, `Subject: Re: <original verbatim>`, plus threadId. Integration test asserts single-thread rendering in a non-Gmail client (Outlook.com).
- **Duplicate or lost sends from double-click, timeout retry, or crash mid-send** (critical, seen by 3 lenses) — Gmail accepts the message but the process dies before the DB commit (double-send on retry), or the 200 response's threadId is never persisted (reply-blind forever). → Solution: status column as idempotency key — `approved → sending` only via compare-and-swap (0 rows = abort, includes the two-tabs case: 409 → "Sent from another device"); persist our Message-ID *before* calling send; never blind-retry — probe `q='rfc822msgid:<id>'` first; a repair sweep resolves rows stuck in `sending` by the same probe (found → backfill sent + threadId; absent after 24h → revert to approved). Unique index on (person_id, step).
- **Token revoked mid-campaign while sequences are live** (high, seen by 3 lenses) — user revokes from myaccount.google.com (or a scared cousin does); polling goes blind while countdowns keep running, risking follow-ups onto replied threads. → Solution: hard invariant — no send while `last_successful_poll_at` is older than 6 hours; on `invalid_grant`, freeze drafting/sending but keep deriving dates forward. On reconnect: full reply sweep first, then reconcile the backlog — re-spread overdue items across working days respecting the ceiling and same-domain spacing (no 10-email burst), regenerate anything >2 working days stale. The reconnect screen doubles as a plain-language privacy explainer written for the skeptical cousin.
- **Reconnect with the wrong Google account corrupts all state** (high) — account-chooser defaults to the other signed-in Gmail; OAuth succeeds but every stored threadId belongs to the old mailbox. → Solution: store the connected address from `users.getProfile` at first connect; on reconnect, compare and refuse mismatches, re-launching OAuth with `login_hint=<original>`. A separate explicit "switch account" action warns that live threads will be archived.
- **User acts manually in Gmail and the robot contradicts them** (high, seen by 2 lenses) — user replies to a recipient from the Gmail app, or copy-pastes and sends a draft themselves; 4 days later the tool sends "just checking you saw this" into a warm thread, or re-surfaces an already-sent email. → Solution: poller reads whole threads — any message authored by the user's own address (or aliases) that isn't a stored messageId → sequence `user_took_over`, queued follow-ups cancelled, dashboard offers "resume tracking?". Also scan the Sent folder for messages to tracked contacts (mark sent, start countdown from the real date) and, at company-add time, for prior contact with the domain ("you emailed them in June — follow up or skip?").
- **Genuine reply lands in the user's SPAM folder and polling misses it** (high) — `messages.list` defaults exclude SPAM/TRASH; the breakup fires at the one person who answered. → Solution: poll via `threads.get` on stored threadIds (returns SPAM/TRASH-labeled messages) or pass `includeSpamTrash=true`. A SPAM-labeled reply still stops the sequence and surfaces with a "found in your spam — mark Not Spam" deep link (also trains Gmail).
- **Hard vs soft bounce both look like "a reply from mailer-daemon"** (high, seen by 2 lenses) — treating any thread activity as a reply shows a happy badge on a dead address; treating every DSN as hard burns real contacts on a mailbox-full. → Solution: DSN detection (mailer-daemon/postmaster + multipart/report), parse the Status field: 5.x.x → `email_status=bounced`, cancel the person's steps, log the departure signal, rotate the ladder with cooldown waived (nobody saw anything) but same-domain spacing kept; 4.x.x → hold and retry once after 1 working day. User-facing framing is always calm: "That address wasn't active — usually means they changed jobs; moving to the next contact," never raw SMTP jargon.
- **historyId expiry and quota storms break polling** (medium) — after a quiet Eid week `history.list` 404s; a naive fallback re-fetches everything and hits 429 storms. → Solution: on 404, full resync limited to threads with non-terminal outreach, then store a fresh historyId; one Gmail wrapper with exponential backoff + jitter and a per-user token bucket; poll every 10–15 minutes, never per-minute — the SLA is measured in working days.

### Reply Classification & Inbound Handling

- **Every inbound message must be classified before it can change state** (critical, umbrella rule) — the plan's "any reply stops the sequence" is wrong for most real inbound. → Solution: one Claude classifier over every thread message, categories: `human_positive | neutral_question | rejection_hard | rejection_soft | removal_request | referral | auto_reply_ooo | auto_ack_unmonitored | gateway_challenge | departed | complaint_escalation | provenance_challenge | prior_contact_callout | bounce`. Header pre-checks (Auto-Submitted, X-Autoreply, Precedence, multipart/report) run first. Ambiguous → default `human_reply` and surface the thread: a false stop is cheap, a false bump is not.
- **Referral reply gets torched by the ladder** (critical, seen by 3 lenses) — "looping in Sara who runs our Nafis program" (or a CC'd colleague) is already ladder rank 2, and days later she gets a cold email from someone she was just warm-introduced to. → Solution: parse From/To/CC of every inbound against the person table (email + domain/name fuzzy match); matches get `in_warm_thread=true` — a hard block on cold-sequence start; unmatched addresses at the domain become person rows (`source=referral`, the warmest contact class, ranked above the ladder). On referral: all company outreach → `paused_referral`, pre-draft a warm reply in the existing thread, and the company exits that state only by user action, never by countdown.
- **"Remove me" must suppress the domain, not the person** (critical, seen by 3 lenses) — sequence closes, but 5 days later the ladder cold-emails her colleague two desks away; she forwards to IT and the user's personal Gmail gets domain-flagged. → Solution: global suppression table (email_hash, domain, scope, reason, date) checked before any draft generation, permanent, surviving dormancy resets and shared across all future users. `removal_request` → person suppressed + company `suppressed_by_request` (blocks rotation and reactivation; override only via explicit warning modal). Pre-draft a one-line confirmation reply; exclude these from reply-rate metrics. Never surfaced as a red failure or above positive items — the emotional framing is "Not a fit — closed and moved on; this is how the numbers work."
- **A rejection must kill the whole company ladder, not just the person** (critical) — "our quota is met this year" is recorded as terminal `replied`; after cooldown the HR lead gets emailed and walks over: "didn't you already tell this guy no?" → Solution: `rejection_hard` → company `dormant_until` +90 days immediately, all ladder rows frozen, thank-you reply pre-drafted. `rejection_soft` with a timeframe → Claude extracts it, sets `dormant_until`, and pre-schedules a re-approach that honestly references the exchange ("you kindly suggested Q4"). Invariant: ladder rotation is only legal after silence, never after any classified rejection.
- **Out-of-office counted as a real reply** (critical, seen by 3 lenses) — an Eid vacation responder stops the sequence forever and shows a celebratory "Replied"; this happens in bulk around every Eid. → Solution: `auto_reply_ooo` (tested against Arabic/bilingual OOO) is not a reply and not a touch; parse the return date and reschedule the next step to return + 2 UAE working days with a naturally regenerated opener ("hope you had a good break"); no parseable date → pause 5 working days; if the shift blows past the breakup step, stretch the cadence rather than closing. Dashboard shows "OOO until ~Aug 18", never "replied".
- **Late reply lands after the sequence closed and the company went dormant** (critical, seen by 2 lenses) — three weeks post-breakup: "we just opened two Nafis-track roles — still interested?" is invisible if polling only watches active sequences, while person 2 may be mid-sequence at the same company. → Solution: poll the thread IDs of every message ever sent, forever (the set is small). A late reply reopens the person as `replied`, un-dormants the company, freezes any other live sequence there (`paused_late_reply`) with a banner, and pushes an urgent pre-drafted response to the top of the Review queue.
- **Escalation from an address we never emailed** (high) — legal-compliance@company.ae replies "we treat this as a PDPL matter"; matching by known person_id drops it while follow-up 1 is still scheduled. → Solution: thread-first matching — anything chaining via References/In-Reply-To to a sent thread attaches to that outreach regardless of sender; create ad-hoc person rows for unknown senders. `complaint_escalation` → domain suppression and all company sequences killed at poll time (not next cron tick), plus a coached one-line apology draft: reply once, never argue.
- **Secure-gateway challenge: the clock runs on an email nobody saw** (high) — Mimecast/Proofpoint (common at UAE banks) quarantines email 1 behind a "verify you are human" link; follow-ups fire into quarantine, then the ladder rotates off the best contact for false silence. → Solution: `gateway_challenge` → pause the countdown, surface the challenge link as a dashboard action, restart Day-0 only after the user confirms completion; record `gateway=true` on the company for later ladder sends. Uncompleted after 5 working days → treat as soft bounce and rotate.
- **"How did you get my email?"** (high) — addresses are pattern-guessed; a vague answer reads as evasive and escalates toward the PDPL complaint. → Solution: `provenance_challenge` → halt the sequence and pre-draft an honest answer assembled from the stored `source_urls` and email derivation ("saw your role on <page>, used your company's standard format — apologies if unwelcome"). After answering, person → no-further-followups. Honesty converts some into conversations; evasion converts all into spam reports.
- **Recipient replied on LinkedIn or by phone — the system sees silence** (high) — no LinkedIn integration by design, so follow-up 1 fires while they're already chatting, which reads as a bot. → Solution: (1) "They replied elsewhere" action on every person row → `replied_external`, countdowns halt; (2) because lazy users won't volunteer it, every follow-up Review card leads with an interlock: "Any contact from Khalid outside email? [No — send] [Yes — stop]". Onboarding coaches: accept the connection, steer substance back to email.
- **Departed contact — human or automated "no longer with us"** (medium, seen by 2 lenses) — "Khalid left Alpha Bank; contact talent@..." classified as a generic reply ends in false success, and his stale evidence keeps feeding drafts. → Solution: `departed` → person status permanent `departed`, `email_status=invalid`, all evidence keyed to them flagged stale (generator's refuse rule excludes it). Extract any named successor → new person row atop the ladder, eligible for a near-immediate email that honestly references the redirect. Hard NDRs ("mailbox disabled") route to the same path.
- **Auto-ack from a ticketing or unmonitored mailbox** (medium) — the sourced "Emiratisation lead" is emiratisation@ behind an ATS; counting it as a reply is false success, ignoring it harvests ticket numbers. → Solution: `auto_ack_unmonitored` (no return date, mentions tickets/portals) → not a reply, not a touch; person closed `dead_end_mailbox`, ladder advances after a 2-working-day gap (no human saw anything, full cooldown wasted); surface the extracted portal URL as a manual to-do; mark the address `role_based=true` so sourcing down-ranks generic prefixes.
- **Person 2 calls out the email to person 1** (medium) — "you also wrote to Ahmed last month — why me now?"; a dodging draft gets the candidate caught being strategically dishonest in a small office. → Solution: generator receives `prior_contact_at_company=true` for every ladder step > 1 and is contract-forbidden from first-contact phrasing; `prior_contact_callout` → pre-draft the honest pivot ("I wrote to Ahmed because I wasn't sure who owns Emiratisation hiring — it looks like that's you"). If person 2 forwards to person 1, treat it as a referral that warm-reopens person 1's closed state.
- **Arabic inbound breaks classification** (medium) — an Arabic polite rejection classified as neutral engagement triggers an enthusiastic English reply to a "no". → Solution: the classifier prompt carries Arabic and mixed-language few-shot examples for every category; store a `language` field; Review cards show a machine translation of the inbound so the user understands what they're approving; drafted responses stay English but acknowledge naturally.

### Scheduler & State Machine

- **Reply races a queued follow-up sitting in the Review queue** (critical) — the contact says "happy to chat" at 8:40; the user sends the pre-drafted bump at 9:05. → Solution: two guards. Poller: on any counterpart message, atomically flip every queued/drafted/approved outreach for that person to terminal `superseded_by_reply` in the same transaction. Send-time gate: inside the `approved→sending` CAS, fetch the thread and compare the latest message id against the last recorded; any unseen inbound aborts the send with "They replied — read it first." The poll race can lose; the send gate cannot.
- **Follow-up whose predecessor never sent** (high, seen by 2 lenses) — user disappears 10 days; follow-up 2 comes due while follow-up 1 sits unsent, producing "following up on my note" for a note that never existed. → Solution: state-machine invariant — a sequence advances only after a confirmed send; a follow-up with an unsent predecessor cannot exist. Drafted-but-unsent items expire to `stale` after 3 working days (first emails) / 2 (follow-ups) and regenerate on next surfacing.
- **All date math must be Asia/Dubai calendar dates** (high) — a UTC server anchors day-0 to the wrong day and the same-day-per-company check compares different days than recipients experience. → Solution: every date the state machine reasons about (anchors, due dates, spacing, ceiling reset, sending window) is a UAE date from converting the instant to Asia/Dubai (fixed UTC+4, no DST) before truncating; timestamps store UTC, date columns store UAE dates; unit tests run with TZ=America/New_York. Companion canonical rule for weekend sends: one pure function `nextDue(sentDateUAE, k, calendar)` — anchor = send date if working day else next working day, due = k-th working day strictly after — table-driven tests for weekend/holiday-adjacent/on-holiday sends; no other date arithmetic allowed in the codebase.
- **scheduled_date is derived, never authoritative — moon-sighting and retro holiday changes** (high, seen by 3 lenses) — Eid shifts a day on moon-sighting; the government adds a day off two days out; pre-drafted "Eid Mubarak" fires before Eid or a week after. → Solution: nightly (and on calendar edit) recompute due dates from (prior send date, working-day count, current calendar); unconfirmed windows count as fully non-working. Holiday openers bind at send-eligibility time, not draft time: drafts crossing an unconfirmed window carry `regenerate_at_send`; founder gets a "confirm Eid dates" task 3 days before each window; unconfirmed by send day → generic opener (correct beats warm-but-wrong). Calendar edits: dates move later freely, earlier only to tomorrow at soonest; `calendar_version` stamp makes stale openers detectable.
- **Person 1 replies after person 2's sequence started** (high) — the company now has two live threads and person 2's follow-up is queued. → Solution: company state `reply_conflict`: person 1 `closed → replied` (a reply always wins), person 2 → `paused_pending_reply`, all their queued steps on hold in one transaction. Dashboard forces a choice: "going with person 1" → person 2 `closed_silent` (only email 1 sent; never send a retraction); "person 1 was a dead end" → person 2 resumes with recomputed dates through the stale-redraft rule. "One live sequence per company" enforced as an invariant check at every send.
- **The scheduler must be a stateless idempotent sweep, never a tick** (high) — a 3-day server outage silently drops every follow-up whose fire-moment fell inside it. → Solution: each run executes "for every non-terminal outreach where derived_due_date ≤ today(Dubai) and no next-step row exists, create/refresh the draft; for every sequence past cooldown, advance the ladder." Running twice is a no-op (unique (person_id, step) index); running late produces the same rows, which flow through the stale-redraft rule. Nothing may depend on a job firing at a particular moment.
- **Post-gap backlog vs the daily ceiling** (high) — Monday after Eid: 14 follow-ups due + 20 first emails vs a 12/day ceiling; either cold outreach stalls or 34 emails fire in one morning. → Solution: two budgets — follow-ups (own cap ~20) always drain first FIFO by original due date; first emails use the 10–15 ceiling only after; combined hard cap ~25/day; unused budget never rolls over. Same-domain spacing applied after budgeting, deferring the loser a day. Queue UI shows the split ("14 follow-ups, 6 new — 8 deferred").
- **Queue-open freshness pass after user absence** (high, seen by 2 lenses) — the returning user batch-sends a queue containing replied threads, a bounce, and "since my note last week" that is two weeks wrong. → Solution: before any draft renders sendable: (1) full reply/bounce sweep, supersede accordingly; (2) drafts >2 working days past scheduled_date → `stale`, regenerated with correct temporal references; (3) show a "Welcome back — we paused everything; here are today's 3" triage screen, never the full backlog. Sends blocked until the pass completes.
- **Sibling companies under one domain defeat per-company rules** (medium) — "Emirates NBD" and "Emirates NBD Capital" as separate rows both resolve @emiratesnbd.com; two people in one real org get cold email the same morning. → Solution: all sequencing invariants (one live sequence, same-day spacing, cooldown, dormancy) enforce at an `org_group` keyed by normalized email domain (manual parent links for distinct-domain subsidiaries); company insert de-duplicates on domain with a "same as existing?" prompt.
- **Global pause and "I got the job" controls** (medium, seen by 3 lenses) — the user accepts an offer (or hits exams/a wedding) while 6 follow-ups are queued; firing them, or ghosting warm threads, burns the small UAE network and never records the headline metric. → Solution: one-tap Pause (`user.paused` freezes the sweep; resume recomputes dates via the stale rule). "I got the job": bulk-close silent early sequences (`closed_won_silent`, no breakups), pre-draft courteous withdrawals for `in_conversation` threads through the normal Review queue, archive the rest, set `placed(date)`, and log time-to-placement, sends-to-placement, and the winning contact_type/evidence_tier — the pitch-deck numbers.

### Review & Send

- **A real reply is the product's climax and the user freezes** (critical, seen by 3 lenses) — the interview invite arrives, the user panics, "does it tomorrow" for four days, and the lead moves on. → Solution: replies are queue items, not exits. `replied` spawns a reply-assist card in the same Review & Send queue: Claude classifies and pre-drafts a response from the full thread + profile + evidence under the same clarify-and-refuse contract (facts only from the confirmed profile; missing facts emit a one-tap question to the user instead of invention). Reply-SLA countdown of 1 working day, shown in hours, with an out-of-band push/email nudge — the user sees the manager's reply in Gmail before opening the app, so the coach must reach them there. Profile interview therefore captures Nafis registration, notice period, and availability as structured fields.
- **Mobile: the split-view evidence panel never gets seen** (high) — the target user is phone-only; the panel stacks below the fold and the one safety mechanism against personalization errors goes unused. → Solution: mobile-first — personalized claims highlighted inline in the draft; tapping opens a bottom sheet with quote + source. First view of any draft requires one interaction (scroll past highlights or tap the top evidence chip) before Send activates — a 2-second speed bump. Desktop keeps the split view.
- **First-send courage gap** (high) — onboarding succeeds, the first draft is good, and the user never presses Send; analytics shows silent churn between "viewed" and "sent". → Solution: the first surfaced draft is always the safest in the queue — an Emiratisation lead with the strongest evidence, labeled "this person's job is to want this email"; highlight one verbatim phrase from the user's own interview ("in your words"); small win screen after send #1; one reframing nudge if a viewed first draft sits 48h ("worst case: nothing lost").
- **SQLite is the only draft store — Gmail Drafts is never used** (high) — drafts created via `gmail.compose` get edited or deleted by the user in Gmail; the Review screen then shows a body that won't send, annotated by evidence for text that no longer exists. → Solution: design rule — drafts exist only as outreach rows; the MIME is constructed at click time from the SQLite copy. Kills the divergence class, drops the `gmail.compose` scope (shrinking the CASA audit), and keeps evidence-ID linkage valid because the annotated body is the sent body. Store the final sent text verbatim — it, not the generated text, is what follow-up generation and dispute forensics read.
- **Bad edits: hook deleted, claims invented, 400 words** (medium, seen by 2 lenses) — the user pastes in three passion paragraphs, deletes the evidence hook, and adds "I've applied through your portal" (false). → Solution: never block edits; run a pre-send soft lint on edited drafts — length warning over ~150 words, "you removed the personal opening" on hook deletion, and a diff of edits against evidence-backed text flagging unbacked new factual claims ("this sentence isn't backed by your profile or evidence — send anyway?"). One-tap restore, one-tap send-anyway.
- **The 40-approval Saturday-night binge** (medium) — a motivated user hammers Send 40 times, blowing the ceiling and emailing at 11pm Saturday Gulf. → Solution: the Send button is governed by the invariants, not user stamina: past today's budget, drafts render with Send disabled ("unlocks tomorrow 9:00 Gulf") and stay `drafted` — no hidden auto-send queue (hard rule 1). Same-domain spacing disables the second same-company draft. Off-hours sends get a one-line confirm but are not blocked. No "Approve all" button exists, deliberately.
- **No attachments on any cold-sequence step** (low) — the plan bans them on email 1 only; a CV PDF on the breakup email is a top gateway-quarantine trigger on the one email that's the last shot. → Solution: the MIME builder for outreach sends simply has no attachment path; attachments exist only in reply-assist (warm threads, where the CV request arrives). Review-screen copy: "They'll ask for your CV — that's the goal of this email, not its payload."

### Generation

- **Refusals must be invisible upstream and actionable downstream — never an empty queue** (high, seen by 2 lenses) — the clarify-&-refuse contract correctly refuses 6 of 8 thin contacts and the daily-habit user sees a dead product (or jargon about "collection requests"). → Solution: keep a buffer invariant — the sourcing pipeline maintains ≥2 days of fully-evidenced drafts ahead of the ceiling, alerting the operator (never the user) below that. When a refusal does need the user, render it as a "Needs one fact" card with the generator's specific request and a paste-a-URL box that structures evidence inline; after 5 unresolved working days offer a labeled lower-odds generic draft or one-click skip. Empty states always say something true and forward-looking.
- **Nafis/policy figures go stale mid-campaign** (high, seen by 2 lenses) — a queued follow-up quotes last year's AED 7,000 tier to the one person guaranteed to know the current rules. → Solution: v1 templates never state amounts or thresholds ("Nafis salary support applies" is maximum specificity; numbers only if the user types them). Any policy claim lives as an evidence row keyed to the official Nafis/MOHRE page with `verified_on`; the scheduler re-fetches rows older than 30 days before releasing a citing draft and regenerates on drift; the generator refuses facts older than 6 months.
- **Real evidence that is creepy or embarrassing to cite** (high) — the highest-tier item is a post about the target's bereavement or a political controversy, and "use highest-tier evidence" opens the email with it. → Solution: sensitivity gate at evidence structuring — Claude assigns topic labels; a hard blocklist (health, bereavement, family, religion beyond neutral greetings, politics, legal disputes, layoffs) sets `usable=false` so tier-ranking picks the best usable fact. Blocked rows appear greyed in the panel with the reason, so the user understands why the hook is the second-best fact.
- **Long pause turns a bump into an absurdity** (medium, seen by 2 lenses) — the late-Ramadan pause stretches "+4 working days" into 18 calendar days; "following up on last week's note" is nonsense. → Solution: when the gap between step n's send and step n+1's due date exceeds 10 working days, regenerate in re-introduction mode (recap sender + original hook + holiday warmth) — the generator receives `gap_working_days`. Also stop feeding new email-1 drafts when <4 working days remain before a known pause window, so sequences aren't born straddling it.
- **Role titles are targeting metadata, not claims** (medium) — "as Head of Talent Acquisition, you..." to someone promoted to CHRO reads as drive-by research. → Solution: rule — role_title is never an evidence row, so the contract forbids asserting it in body text; greetings use the name only. At draft time, freshness_date > 90 days shows a one-click re-check that re-runs the Tier-3 search and re-ranks the ladder on a title change.
- **Honorifics: Sheikh/Dr./H.E. wrongly applied or omitted** (medium) — first_name='Dr.', or 'Sheikha' the given name greeted as royalty; in UAE business culture these read as mockery or ignorance. → Solution: parse honorifics into a separate field at ingest with a strip-list; "Sheikha" + family-name pattern is a given name, never a title. Default greeting is the plain first name; honorifics apply only when confirmed by an official source, and Sheikh/H.E. require a manual confirmation checkbox. The Review pane highlights any auto-applied honorific.
- **Arabic evidence the user can't verify in 10 seconds** (medium) — the best hook is an Arabic WAM announcement; the panel shows text the user can't read, or the generator upgrades "participated in" to "led". → Solution: evidence rows carry `language`, `quote`, `quote_translated`; the panel leads with the tagged translation, Arabic beneath. The generator is constrained to the translated text with hedged role verbs ("was part of" unless "led" is explicit). Never splice Arabic into the English body; nuance the translation can't carry → downgrade and emit a collection request.

### Sourcing & Data Integrity

- **Wrong person with the same name gets the draft** (critical) — Tier-3 search binds the person row to a different Ahmed Al Mansoori; the email lands in the real one's inbox reading like a scam. → Solution: identity-binding rule — draft-eligible only with an "anchor source" (a URL naming both the person AND the company/role together); every evidence row must be the anchor or share its identity key (LinkedIn slug, role title). The Review panel pins the anchor ("why we believe this is the right person"). No anchor → `identity_unconfirmed`, excluded from the queue, collection request emitted.
- **Catch-all domains masquerade as verified** (critical) — ZeroBounce returns accept-all; the address is treated as verified, the server swallows all three steps silently, and the reply-rate metric that gates commercialisation is corrupted. → Solution: `accept_all` is a distinct email_status, never collapsed into verified. Sendable only when the exact address was seen verbatim in a public source; otherwise held with a collection request. When one does send: cap at email 1 + one follow-up, badge "delivery unconfirmed," exclude from reply-rate analytics.
- **Staleness rule as schema gate, not guideline** (critical) — a lead who left 4 months ago still verifies (IT offboarding lag); the user burns the company's best evidence and 3 steps on a ghost. → Solution: person rows carry `freshness_date` + `corroborating_source_count`; drafting blocked unless sources ≥ 2 OR freshness < 90 days. Evidence panel shows "last confirmed at company" with date + source. Re-verify the address within 48h of any step-1 send. A mid-sequence bounce = departure signal: `departed`, close without cooldown, release locked evidence, queue the next rung immediately.
- **Transliteration variants create duplicate people and twin sequences** (high) — "Mohammed Al Marri" (Tier 2) and "Mohamed AlMarri" (Tier 3) get two ladder ranks and two live sequences at one company; the wrong email variant bounces. → Solution: normalize at ingest (strip Al-/Bin/Bint, map transliteration families Mohammed/Mohamed/Muhammad/Mohd etc. to a phonetic key); dedupe on (company_id, phonetic_key) with a UI merge prompt; the one-sequence check keys on phonetic identity. Email inference generates and verifies every variant seen in sources plus family siblings, sending only to one returning valid on a non-catch-all domain.
- **The ladder's second email reuses the first email's only hook** (high) — the sole evidence item ("Best SME Bank 2026") hooks email 1 to person 1 and then, after rotation, near-identically hooks person 2 — who compare notes over coffee. → Solution: enforce §4 in the database — on send, evidence_ids get `used_for_person_id` locks scoped to the company; rotation requires ≥1 unlocked evidence row of tier ≤ 4, else rotation is blocked and a collection request fires the moment sequence 1 closes (the cooldown doubles as the re-sourcing window). Nothing new by expiry → "Needs one fact to continue" card, never a rehash.
- **Email pattern inference needs ≥2 exemplars and multi-domain awareness** (high) — the group hires under brand.ae but mails from group.com; one exemplar plus a catch-all blessing makes every contact unreachable while looking green. → Solution: pattern confidence is per-domain and requires ≥2 independent exemplar addresses from public sources; company rows store `candidate_domains[]` and inference prefers the domain exemplars actually use; <2 exemplars caps addresses at `guessed` (collection request, no draft). Review panel shows pattern provenance ("first.last@group.com — pattern from 2 sources").
- **Evidence about the right person's wrong namesake** (high) — the person row is correct but evidence collection pulls a Gulf News quote from a footballer with the same name; the panel shows a real quote with a real link that belongs to someone else. → Solution: entity resolution at evidence-structuring time — every person-level (tier 1–3) row must co-mention the company, the known role, or match the anchor's link identity; Claude outputs an `identity_match` field quoting the matched cue. Failures auto-downgrade to unusable ("namesake risk"); each evidence chip shows its cue ("mentions: ADCB").
- **Exemplar bounce poisons the pattern for the whole ladder** (medium) — person 1's verified first.last@ hard-bounces; persons 2 and 3 are queued on addresses minted from a pattern whose only exemplar just died. → Solution: pattern records list exemplars and live status; a hard bounce on the sole exemplar reverts all sibling pattern-derived addresses to `guessed`, pulls them from the queue, and forces re-verification. Distinguish from departure: bounce + still-live anchor source = wrong address (retry next pattern variant), not gone (don't rotate).
- **Evidence links rot between collection and review** (medium) — the LinkedIn post gets deleted or the leadership page moves; three weeks later the user clicks the evidence link, gets a 404, and trusts nothing. → Solution: snapshot at collection (`context_snippet`, `captured_at`); the panel renders the snapshot as the primary verification surface, live link secondary ("captured 12 Jul"). Nightly HEAD-check marks dead links; a dead-link hook on a queued draft flags it for re-review instead of auto-release; author-deleted posts additionally set `usable=false` — citing a deliberately removed post is its own embarrassment.

### Dashboard & Retention

- **The zero-reply weeks are statistically normal and read as failure** (critical) — the cadence guarantees most replies arrive days 5–12, after follow-ups; the user quits on day 12, right before the wave. → Solution: week 1–2 dashboard runs on leading indicators ("14 companies in play, 6 follow-ups landing this week"); never render a big "0 replies" — show "first replies expected around [date]" with a small timeline until the first reply exists, then switch reply-centric. Set the expectation explicitly at onboarding.
- **Fixed daily drip vs bursty real usage creates a guilt backlog** (high) — the 10–15/day pipeline against 15 minutes/week of actual usage means every session opens with "overdue" evidence of failure — the strongest abandonment driver for this demographic. → Solution: the ceiling is a maximum; the queue generator targets the observed send rate (trailing 7-day sends + 20%). UI rules: no total backlog count, no "overdue" for first emails (only follow-ups have deadlines/countdowns); every session frames a finish line ("Today: 3 emails, ~4 minutes") with a "Done for today" completion state.
- **Recipient-side spam placement is invisible and compounding** (high, seen by 2 lenses) — silent spam-flags degrade the user's *personal* Gmail while the tool keeps sending 15/day into the void. → Solution: engineer the proxies — (1) template lint: plain text, ≤1 link, no shorteners, no attachments; (2) ramp the ceiling in code 3→5→10→15 over two weeks; (3) the breakup email always carries a genuine opt-out sentence, converting silent flags into classifiable removal requests; (4) rolling tripwire: reply rate under half of trailing baseline + rising bounces → auto-throttle to 5/day and surface deliverability guidance (seed-mailbox placement test); (5) enforce never-two-sends-per-domain-within-48h in the scheduler, including across ladder rotations.
- **Positive reply, then user silence — the engine only chases pre-reply** (medium) — "ping me next week" hits terminal `replied` and the warmest lead evaporates while the dashboard shows success. → Solution: post-reply state — positive threads get a nudge countdown on the same working-day engine (default +5 working days, or the date Claude extracts); at zero, a gentle pre-drafted nudge quoting their own words enters the normal Review queue. Next Actions always lists warm threads above cold follow-ups — warm beats cold.
- **Ramadan/Eid pause looks like a dead product** (medium, seen by 2 lenses) — the queue empties, countdowns freeze, nothing explains why, and the habit breaks over the holiday. → Solution: any global pause renders an explicit full-width state ("Paused until after Eid — recruiters aren't reading either; resumes [date] with Eid openers ready") and routes the motivation burst to non-send tasks (pre-approve drafts, review the CV, skip-list grooming). Resume day sends a re-engagement nudge ("Eid Mubarak — 4 emails ready").
- **Dormancy override pressure: "can we email Emirates NBD again?"** (medium) — a flat "no" makes the user route around the tool via untracked manual Gmail. → Solution: show, don't just block — the company card displays full history and the dormancy countdown. Early re-entry allowed only with a new trigger (fresh job posting or new tier ≤ 2 evidence collected after dormancy began) and only to a never-contacted person, logged with the trigger evidence ID. Otherwise offer "front of the queue on the day dormancy ends."

### Business & Compliance

- **Restricted Gmail scopes are a commercialisation time-bomb** (critical, seen by 3 lenses) — gmail.send/readonly require Google verification plus an annual CASA security assessment (months of lead time, real money); unverified caps at 100 users behind a scare wall, and meanwhile the founder technically holds read access to users' entire inboxes. → Solution: minimize the audit surface now — two scopes only (compose dropped), poll only tool-created threads by stored IDs (never broad inbox queries), persist only tool-thread reply content, encrypt tokens at rest, and write the one-page data-access statement today. "Start verification + CASA" is a funded week-1 line item of commercialisation with 2–3 months lead, before paying user #2.
- **UAE PDPL exposure from storing non-consenting third parties** (critical) — names, inferred emails, and scraped quotes about hundreds of professionals is personal-data processing under Decree-Law 45/2021 (stricter still for DIFC/ADGM targets — exactly where Tier 1 is strongest), and one inexecutable erasure request becomes a complaint. → Solution: v1 posture — public business-contact data only, minimal fields (never phone, photo, nationality guesses), SQLite local, retention purge N months after company `exhausted`. Build the cheap-now erasure mechanic: "forget person" cascades soft-deletes through person → evidence → outreach, redacts sent copies, keeps an email-hash in suppression so they can't be re-sourced. Budget (don't build) the commercial-phase items: privacy notice, legitimate-interest analysis, DSR workflow.
- **The schema must be global-keyed or the collision-moat ledger can never exist** (high) — month 8, two clients cold-email the same Emiratisation lead nine days apart on the same LinkedIn-post hook; the cross-client ledger PLAN §12 calls the moat can't be retrofitted onto a single-user namespace. → Solution: even with one user, `company` (canonical domain unique) and `person` (normalized email unique) are user-agnostic; per-user state lives in `user_company_state` and `outreach`. Write a global `contact_ledger(person_id, user_id, sent_at, evidence_ids[])` on every send — redundant with one user, which is the point — and give evidence rows `locked_by_user_id, locked_until` so cross-client spacing and distinct-hook enforcement drop in for free.
- **Interview scheduling handoff breaks the state machine** (high) — "Tuesday 3pm at our DIFC office?" ping-pongs in Gmail; the machine only knows `replied`, cooldown expires, and person 2 gets a cold email at the company the user visits Tuesday. → Solution: a positive reply freezes the whole company (`in_conversation` — blocks rotation and all queued outreach for the domain until manually resolved). No calendar integration in v1: onboarding captures standing availability so reply-assist drafts concrete slots. Explicit outcome buttons — "Interview booked" (records date, `won_pending`, schedules a day-after debrief + thank-you draft) and "Conversation ended" — which are also the telemetry events pricing depends on.
- **Founder hand-fixes bad data and corrupts live state** (high) — an 11pm raw sqlite3 edit to a wrong role silently breaks the scheduled_date invariant and the stale evidence sends anyway. → Solution: guarded admin scripts only (`fix-person`, `kill-draft`, `requeue`, `suppress`, `forget-person`), each writing the append-only log and recomputing dependent state (fixing a person invalidates drafts referencing its evidence IDs). Never raw edits on tables the scheduler reads. User side: a "this is wrong" flag on the evidence panel pulls the draft, marks the row `disputed`, and creates a founder task — the user's only job is noticing.
- **The friend phase is the only cheap unit-economics dataset ever** (medium) — six months later pricing needs founder-minutes-per-send and nobody logged the 40 minutes of manual sourcing behind each email. → Solution: formalize log event names from send #1 — `evidence_collected(tier, minutes_spent)`, `draft_generated/refused/edited`, `sent(step, contact_type, evidence_tiers)`, `reply(sentiment, hours_to_reply)`, `interview_booked`, `placed` — with a start/stop timer or rough-duration picker on the capture form, because founder labor is the real COGS. The three pricing numbers (minutes/send, sends/positive-reply, replies/interview) must be one query by week 6.

## 6. Build Order & Definition of Done

Follow `PLAN.md` §11 week by week. A milestone is done only when its user-POV acceptance tests pass and its edge-case items are handled or logged as deferred in `EDGE_CASES.md`.

### Week 1 — Skeleton + onboarding
Next.js app, full SQLite schema (§4 above, global-keyed from day one), Google OAuth + Gmail connect (production publishing status, two scopes, granted-scope check, pre-consent explainer, identity block), profile interview flow with the chip UI + micro-follow-up + hygiene questions + CV step, UAE calendar table with holiday windows and `confirmed` flags. Seed the friend's profile and 30–50 companies with ranked ladders.
**Done when:**
- My tech-shy friend connects Gmail and finishes the interview in under 10 minutes, on their phone, without asking me anything — including surviving the Google consent screen without closing the tab.
- Unchecking the send scope at consent produces a plain-language re-launch, not a completed onboarding.
- The minimum-viable-profile gate rejects one-word answers with exactly one concrete follow-up per thin field.
- The calendar table round-trips: I can mark an Eid window unconfirmed, edit it, and every derived date recomputes.

### Week 2 — Sourcing tooling
Capture form (Tiers 2–4, with minutes-spent picker), Tier-3 search helper, email pattern inference (≥2 exemplars, per-domain, candidate_domains) + verification (`accept_all` distinct), evidence store with structuring (sensitivity labels, identity_match, translation, snapshots), name normalization/dedup, anchor-source identity binding, suppression + blocklist checks at ingest.
**Done when:**
- The founder can source a company end-to-end (people, evidence, verified addresses) in one sitting with every minute logged, and a deliberately-planted namesake, transliteration twin, and catch-all domain all get caught by the gates, not by luck.
- No person without an anchor source can reach draft-eligible state (test asserts `identity_unconfirmed` exclusion).

### Week 3 — Generate + Review screen
Clarify-&-refuse generator (evidence contract, banned-phrase lint, honorific rules, intro blocks, sensitivity-filtered tier ranking), Review & Send screen mobile-first (inline highlights + bottom sheet, evidence-interaction speed bump, soft edit lint, budget-governed Send button), idempotent send path (CAS, pre-persisted Message-ID, repair sweep), SQLite-only drafts, "Needs one fact" cards. First 10 real sends with the friend, calibrating voice.
**Done when:**
