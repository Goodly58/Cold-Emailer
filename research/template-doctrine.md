# PART 1 — WHAT BECC HOLLAND ACTUALLY TEACHES

Becc Holland: CEO/founder of **Flip the Script**; previously Head of Sales Development at Chorus.ai, sales leadership at Gong and G2. [SOURCED]

### 1.1 Core philosophy: personalization must be hooked to relevance
Personalization and relevance are different things and **either alone fails**: personalization without relevance → "they may like you but won't buy"; relevance without personalization → mass-blast templates get ignored. [SOURCED — flipthescript.com/personalization-to-relevance-webinar]
Her enforcement rule (the **pizza rule**): *"If you are going to personalize to me by addressing my love for pizza, you'd better be prepared to justify how your product relates to pizza."* [SINGLE-SOURCE — signum.ai] → **The observable you open with must earn its place by connecting to the ask. Decoration is a sin.**

### 1.2 The premise buckets (her taxonomy of observables) [SOURCED]
1. **Self-authored content** — posts, articles, webinars, talks, podcasts
2. **Engaged content** — what they liked, shared, commented on
3. **Self-attributed traits** — headline, About section, how they describe their remit
4. **Junk drawer** — schools, personal interests, hobbies (her warning: "don't go into too personal details — annoying, offensive, or even creepy")
5. **Company** — M&A, company posts, hiring, website language

**Her ranking:** *"the top 3 most responded to premises come from content they authored, content they engaged with, and self-attributed traits."* [SOURCED — signum.ai]
(A "6 Buckets" video exists; the sixth bucket's contents are UNVERIFIED.)

### 1.3 Email architecture — the four lines [SOURCED — 30MPC ep. 31; her LinkedIn cold-email teardown]
> **Premise → Hook → CTA → Push/Pull. That's it.**
- **Premise (line 1):** the opener and **the longest part of the email** — your reason for writing, as personalized as possible. Load-bearing.
- **Hook (line 2):** maps what you offer onto the premise. Her words: **"the least valuable real estate in the email."** 1–2 sentences max.
- **CTA (line 3):** a request to unpack the thing you just mapped, "without the bells and whistles."
- **Push/Pull (line 4):** a release-valve that de-pressurizes the ask (e.g. "either way, keep creating such useful content"). [Labels contested between sources; the *function* — lower the stakes, give a graceful out — is reliable.]
- **Length: not much longer than 4 sentences.** [SOURCED]

### 1.4 Sequence and follow-up [SOURCED — her LinkedIn sequence post]
Her full sequence: 16 touches over 21 (or 30) business days — 5 emails, 10 cold calls, 2 voicemails, 1 LinkedIn research step (pick 3 premises in under 5 minutes). Emails #1/#3/#4 = **Personalization Heavy**; #2/#5 = **Lite** (no premise).
**Her reported conversion pattern: highest on #1, #2, and #5 — with #5, the break-up email, converting highest of all.** The two emails that convert best after the opener carry the least research: effort is front- and back-loaded, the middle is cheap. This is the highest-leverage import.
- Follow-up #1 = **reply on the original thread**: "Any thoughts?" + a **PS carrying added value**. [SOURCED]
- Follow-up #2 = new email, fresh premise. [SINGLE-SOURCE]
- Break-up email: close outreach "in a high-value way" so they "remember you positively while moving on." [SOURCED]

### 1.5 Her banned-language list [SOURCED — "49 words to avoid" LinkedIn post; ~26 retrieved]
> Hope you're well · Happy Tuesday! · I know you're busy but · To see if we're a fit · I'd love to · I'll be brief · Quick question · Just checking in · Just following up · Just circling back · We're the #1 · If this is relevant to you · Did you get my last email?? · Cheers · **Pick your brain** · **Virtual coffee / e-meet** · I don't want to waste your time · **I was really impressed by your profile** · Chat · We work with leaders like you · **Quick call** · **Let's connect** · ROI · **I'd love to network** · Just touching base · Just wondering

Five of these are exactly what job-seekers reach for by default (bolded). Her list is *more* applicable to job outreach than to sales.

# PART 2 — WHAT TRANSFERS, WHAT DOESN'T

**Transfers cleanly:** premise = longest, most personal line (proof-of-effort — a student has more effort than a vendor); the pizza rule (stronger here); the bucket ranking (domain-independent); "least valuable real estate" for self-description (students over-invest in it); the 4-sentence ceiling (hiring managers read on phones); the Push/Pull release line (status asymmetry is larger — the out must be explicit); heavy/lite alternation and the break-up finding; the 49 banned words.

**Does not transfer — cut:** the 10 cold calls and 2 voicemails (phoning a UAE hiring manager cold is a status violation); 16 touches (a candidate who chases like a vendor reads desperate → **3 touches over ~15 business days**); customer case-study CTAs; competitive-displacement plays. **Bucket 4 inverts:** for a student, **shared institution is a credential, not junk-drawer creep** — promote school overlap to near the top; keep hobbies banned.

**The UAE-specific adaptation Holland has no equivalent for:** the sender's Emirati nationality is not a diversity note — **it is the hook**. Under Emiratisation, mainland 50+ companies must hit 10% skilled-Emirati by end-2026 with ~AED 108k/yr penalties per unfilled slot, and Nafis subsidizes the hire; Nafis was extended to 2040 in April 2026 [secondary sources — recheck figures against MoHRE/Nafis before launch]. In Holland's terms, **the sender is structurally relevant before writing a word** — quietly with line managers, explicitly with HR/Emiratisation leads.

Register note: UAE business email runs formal and title-respecting [SINGLE-SOURCE] — plain-voice yes, casual familiarity no. (Full register matrix comes from the culture research.)

# PART 3 — SUBJECT LINES: THE EVIDENCE (all third-party; treat as hypotheses to test)

- 2–4 word subjects → highest opens (~46%) [belkins.io]; a competing dataset says 4–7 words / 36–50 chars [klenty.com] → honest read: **"short" (2–7 words), test the optimum**.
- Personalized subjects: 46% vs 35% open, 7% vs 3% reply [belkins.io].
- Student cold-email corpus (vendor-published, treat with suspicion): 74.7% opens with formulas like "[Name] - [University] student, quick question about [team]".
- Mechanism: information-gap theory — "UCL student" names a category and withholds the reason.
- Company name in subject: +21.9% open [juicebox.ai].
- **Test discipline: ≥200–300 sends per variant**; fix deliverability first; **judge on reply rate, not opens** (image-proxy prefetch corrupts opens).

The founder's finding is consistent with the literature and mechanistically explicable. Ship as a tested default, not doctrine.

# PART 4 — TEMPLATE DOCTRINE (the spec; [ADAPTATION] unless restating a cited rule)

## (a) The personalization pyramid — six tiers

Open with the **highest tier holding a real, recent, specific artifact**. Never mix two tiers in one opener.

- **T1 — What they made** (talk, post, article, podcast, paper). Opener proves consumption: name the *argument*, not the artifact. "Your talk argued X" beats "I watched your talk."
- **T2 — What they backed** (commented/shared with their own framing). Quote their added framing; if they added no words, it's T3 at best.
- **T3 — What they claim about themselves** (headline, About, "we're hiring" post, how they phrase their remit). Reflect their own language back. Always available.
- **T4 — What happened to them** (promotion, award, speaking slot). Weakest person-level tier — every tool congratulates promotions. Only if <30 days old and you add a real question.
- **T5 — What the company did** (results, expansion, Emiratisation milestone, revealing open req). Legitimate opener only when the event directly creates the thing you're asking about; otherwise demote to the hook line.
- **T6 — Junk drawer** (hobbies, sports, hometown). **Banned as an opener.** Sole exception: **shared institution** (university, programme, professor, competition, scholarship cohort) → promoted to T2-equivalent for a student sender.

**Opener rules:**
1. **Recency gate:** T1/T2 older than ~90 days → demote one tier; older than 12 months → two.
2. **Specificity gate:** if you can't state their claim in your own words, you didn't consume it → drop a tier.
3. **Hookability gate (pizza rule):** if you can't write the next sentence connecting observable → ask, **drop the observable even if T1**.
4. **One observable per email.** Two proves you were researching, not reading.
5. **No praise as the verb.** "Impressive / insightful / great post" banned. Agree, disagree, extend, or ask instead.
6. **Fallback ladder for low-signal recipients:** T1 → T2 → T3 → shared institution → T5 → **send the Lite variant instead**. Fabricating a premise is worse than not having one.
7. **Premise gets ~40% of the email's words.**

## (b) Intro-block design — four slots, select at most two

- **Slot A — Identity anchor** (always, 1 clause): "final year finance student at Zayed University".
- **Slot B — Credibility marker** (when the premise raised a technical/domain question): "spent last summer on the settlements team at [Bank]". Must be **adjacent to the premise's subject**; generic markers get cut.
- **Slot C — Status & logistics** (for HR/Emiratisation/recruiters/grad-programme owners; usually **omitted** for a senior line manager on touch 1): "Emirati national, so no sponsorship needed" · "Nafis registered" · "free from June". **State as fact in passing, never as an argument** — do not lecture a UAE HR lead on quotas; they administer them.
- **Slot D — Affinity bridge** (rare; only with a shared-institution or genuine subject-overlap premise): "same programme you did, ten years later". Pizza test, no exceptions.

**Selection matrix:**

| Recipient | Slots | Order |
|---|---|---|
| Line/hiring manager (senior or junior team) | A + B | Identity, then credibility adjacent to premise |
| HR / talent acquisition | A + C | Identity, then status |
| **Emiratisation / Nationalisation lead** | **C + A** | **Status first — it IS the relevance** |
| Graduate programme owner | A + C | Identity, then availability |
| Alum of your university, any role | A + D | Identity, then shared institution |

**Hard caps: intro ≤ 2 sentences, ≤ 30 words.**

## (c) Ask patterns

Interest-based asks beat time-based asks on first touch (asking for time triggers loss aversion) [Gong; Prospeo 304K-email study]. Job-outreach guidance converges: **never ask for a job on email 1; ask for a conversation.**

Rank order for touch 1:
1. **Single question ask** — one specific, answerable, premise-drawn question. Answerable in one line without a calendar.
2. **Bounded time ask** — 15 minutes, their choice, no calendar link. Only after a strong T1/T2 premise.
3. **Redirect ask** — "If someone on your team is closer to this, I would rather ask them." Excellent for senior recipients and UAE hierarchy; delegation still counts as a win.
4. **Permission ask** — "Would it be useful if I sent the two-page version?" Only if the artifact exists.

**Banned asks:** pick your brain · virtual coffee · quick call · let's connect · I'd love to network · let me know if you're hiring · calendar links on touch 1 · CV-review requests on touch 1.
**Rules:** exactly one ask; never stack question + meeting; the ask must be answerable by this recipient personally (no role-design questions to HR, no visa questions to line managers).

**Push/Pull as status equalizer:** "Either way, thank you for putting that talk online." · "If the answer is no, that is a completely fine answer." · "If you would rather point me to someone else, that works too." · "No reply needed if the timing is wrong."
**Never:** "I know you're busy but…", "I don't want to waste your time…", "sorry to bother you" — they lower your status without lowering the ask.

## (d) Follow-up rules — 3 touches, ~15 business days

| Touch | Day | Type | Thread | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Heavy | New | Premise (T1–T3) → Intro (2 slots) → Ask → Push/Pull |
| 2 | +4 | Lite | **Reply, same thread** | "Any thoughts?" + **PS carrying new value**. No new premise. <60 words |
| 3 | +15 | Break-up | **New email, new subject** | Close the loop cleanly, thank for one specific thing, leave one door open, ask for nothing |

1. Every follow-up must contain something new; nothing new → don't send.
2. Never reference the non-response ("did you get my last email" — banned).
3. Never escalate the ask; touch 2 asks the same question smaller.
4. **The PS is where students beat vendors:** actually do the reading, run the number, build the thing. A one-line PS of genuine work is the strongest asset in the sequence.
5. Break-up: no guilt ("I'll assume you're not interested"), no false finality.
6. **Stop at three. Hard cap, not configurable upward.** A fourth touch converts a candidate into a nuisance, and the candidate needs this person's goodwill later.
7. Channel: email only + one optional LinkedIn connection request (no message) on day 0.

## (e) Subject-line families to A/B test (≥200 sends/variant; judge on reply rate)

1. **Bare identity** (founder's finding, primary challenger): `Zayed University student` · `Emirati grad, supply chain`
2. **Identity + company** (tests the +21.9% lift): `Zayed student, [Company]`
3. **Identity + narrow topic:** `UAEU grad, reconciliation`
4. **Premise echo** (highest personalization, lowest scale): `Your Dubai Fintech Summit talk`
5. **Shared institution** (only when true): `Fellow UAEU 2016`
6. **Bare question** (curiosity control): `Year one question`

Never "Re:" on first touch. No exclamation marks. No "Quick question" (banned). No target job title. Touch 3 must change subject.

## (f) Three example emails (under 120 words, plain voice, no em dashes, no banned phrases)

**Example 1 — Line/hiring manager (T1 premise, slots A+B, single-question ask). Subject: `Zayed University student`**

> Your talk at the Dubai Fintech Summit made the case that most of the cost in payments sits in reconciliation, not in the transfers themselves. I went back and read the note you linked afterwards.
>
> I am a final year finance student at Zayed University. I spent last summer on the settlements team at a local bank, mostly working reconciliation exceptions.
>
> Here is what I cannot work out on my own. In year one, is someone with that background more useful inside operations or closer to the product team?
>
> If that is a fifteen minute answer rather than a one line answer, I am happy to call at any time that suits you.
>
> Either way, thank you for putting the talk online.

*(Slot C omitted deliberately: with a senior line manager, nationality is not the point on touch 1.)*

**Example 2 — HR / Emiratisation lead (T5→T2 premise, slots C+A, redirect ask). Subject: `Emirati grad, supply chain`**

> You posted last week that the company hit its Emiratisation target ahead of the December checkpoint, and that the harder problem now is keeping people past year two.
>
> That second part is what I want to ask about. I am an Emirati graduate, supply chain degree from UAEU, class of 2026. Two internships, both in logistics operations at Jebel Ali.
>
> I am not asking you to place me in anything today. I want to understand what makes an Emirati hire stay past year two somewhere like this, so that I choose well the first time.
>
> Fifteen minutes on the phone would help me.
>
> If someone on your team is closer to this, I would rather ask them.

*(Status leads because for this recipient it IS the relevance. "Not asking you to place me" removes the transactional read HR defends against.)*

**Example 3 — Follow-up touch 2 (Lite, reply on thread, PS carries the value). Subject: `Re: Zayed University student`**

> Any thoughts on the question below?
>
> PS. I read the ADGM paper on instant payments that came out this week. The section on reconciliation cost lands almost exactly where you did at the summit, with a number attached. Page thirty one, if you have not got to it.
>
> If the timing is bad, say so and I will stop writing.

## Implementation notes for the tool

1. **Store the pyramid tier of every premise on the record** — tier is the strongest reply-rate predictor available. Report reply rate by tier and let data reorder the pyramid; don't assume the sales ranking holds for UAE job outreach.
2. **Gate on evidence, not availability.** No observable clears the specificity gate → route to the Lite template; never generate a premise. Fabricated premises are the single largest reputational risk in this product.
3. **Enforce caps mechanically:** ≤4 sentences, ≤120 words, ≤2 intro slots, exactly 1 ask, exactly 1 observable.
4. **Ship the 49-word list as a hard blocklist** on generated output — it doubles as an AI-tell filter.
5. **Slot C is a compliance-adjacent claim:** nationality, Nafis registration, sponsorship status come only from the user's verified profile fields, never inferred.
6. **Cap at 3 touches, hard. Not configurable upward.**
7. **Recheck Emiratisation figures before launch** — quota schedule and penalties change by decree.
